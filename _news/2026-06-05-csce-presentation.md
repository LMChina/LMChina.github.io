I gave a presentation titled **"Seismic demand analysis of steel buildings using machine learning-based time-history surrogate model"** in **Technical Session: Machine Learning and AI in Structural Engineering II** at the **Canadian Society of Civil Engineering (CSCE) Annual Conference**.

The presentation focused on efficient probabilistic seismic demand analysis for steel buildings. Seismic demand risk is commonly evaluated by combining conditional demand models with seismic hazard curves, but nonlinear time-history analysis can be computationally expensive when many intensity levels and ground motions are needed. To address this challenge, I presented a physics-informed temporal convolutional network with bidirectional gated recurrent units (**PhyTCN-BiGRU**) as a surrogate model for predicting structural response time histories from ground motion acceleration records.

Using an eight-story steel moment-resisting frame as a demonstration case, the study showed how a surrogate model trained from a limited set of nonlinear analyses can support faster seismic response prediction and probabilistic demand assessment. This work provides a promising pathway toward more efficient performance-based earthquake engineering and design.
